🎯 Problem Statement & Our Approach

The Business Problem

A SaaS subscription company with 20,000 customers and $10.5M in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) is experiencing significant customer churn. The company had no systematic way to:

  • ❌ Identify which customers are about to cancel their subscription
  • ❌ Understand why customers are leaving (is it pricing? support? product usage?)
  • ❌ Quantify how much revenue is at risk in dollar terms
  • ❌ Prioritize which accounts the Customer Success team should call first
  • ❌ Measure whether retention efforts are working
The Cost of Doing Nothing: Without intervention, the company stands to lose $274,093 every month ($3.3M annually) in expected revenue, and $1.07M ARR is directly exposed through 2,484 high-risk accounts.

Our Approach (Step by Step)

1
Data Collection & Validation
Collected 615,628 records across 4 datasets. Validated schema, checked for nulls, duplicates, and referential integrity. Result: Clean data with zero critical issues.
2
SQL Analytics & Exploratory Data Analysis
Wrote 10 SQL analysis scripts for revenue analysis, cohort retention, customer 360 views. Created 15+ visualizations revealing churn patterns by plan, tenure, region.
3
Statistical Hypothesis Testing
Ran Z-tests, T-tests, Chi-squared tests, and 1-way ANOVA to statistically prove which factors actually cause churn (not just correlate). All key drivers showed p < 0.001.
4
Feature Engineering
Engineered 27 predictive features from raw data: usage trends, payment failure rates, CSAT averages, tenure metrics, usage decay ratios. Applied temporal cutoff at June 30, 2025 to prevent data leakage.
5
Machine Learning Model Training
Trained 3 models (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting). Best model: Gradient Boosting Classifier with 0.75 ROC-AUC. Used stratified 80/20 train-test split.
6
Risk Scoring & Business Deliverables
Converted ML probabilities into 0-100 risk scores, 4-tier classification (Low/Medium/High/Critical), root cause driver identification, and dollar-denominated revenue-at-risk calculations.
7
Power BI Executive Dashboard
Built a 3-page interactive dashboard: Executive Overview → Churn Drivers Diagnostics → Customer Success Action Center with dynamic retention playbooks.

💼 Stakeholder Business Questions — Answered

Q1 How much churn is happening?

Our overall churn rate is 33.0% (6,603 out of 20,000 customers). This means roughly 1 in 3 customers either has already churned or is highly likely to churn in the near term. The expected monthly revenue loss across the entire customer base is $274,093 per month, which translates to $3.29M annually.

Q2 Which customer segments churn the most?

Basic Monthly subscribers are the highest-churning segment with an average risk score of 35.2 and 2,393 churned accounts. Monthly billing customers in general churn at 37.5%, compared to only 22.9% for Annual customers. By region, North America (1,610 churners) and Europe (1,131 churners) have the highest absolute churn volumes, proportional to their larger customer bases.

Q3 What behavioral factors are associated with churn?

The top 5 churn drivers, ranked by expected revenue impact:
1. Payment Failure — $204,014/mo loss (74.4%) — 11,714 accounts affected
2. Poor Support Experience — $38,788/mo loss (14.2%) — 2,918 accounts with low CSAT
3. General Attrition — $16,514/mo loss (6.0%) — 2,949 accounts with no single dominant driver
4. Monthly Contract Friction — $14,246/mo loss (5.2%) — 2,335 accounts on month-to-month billing
5. Declining Usage — $532/mo loss (0.2%) — 84 accounts with significant usage drops

Q4 How does churn vary by subscription plan?

Basic Plan: Highest churn count (2,393) with avg risk score 35.2 — lower-priced plans have less switching cost, making customers more likely to leave.
Pro Plan: Moderate churn (1,159) with avg risk score 30.2 — mid-tier shows better retention due to deeper feature engagement.
Enterprise Plan: Lowest churn rate (350) with avg risk score 30.8 — however, each Enterprise churner represents $199/mo loss (13× a Basic customer), making these the most expensive individual losses.

Q5 How does tenure affect churn?

Churn rate and risk score increase with tenure in this dataset:
0-6 months: 432 churned (avg risk 26.1) — Lowest churn; customers are still in the "honeymoon phase"
7-12 months: 699 churned (avg risk 31.0) — Churn starts increasing after the first renewal decision
13-18 months: 892 churned (avg risk 34.5) — Customers who experience cumulative issues start dropping off
19-24 months: 983 churned (avg risk 36.7) — The "fatigue zone" where long-term issues compound
25-30 months: 896 churned (avg risk 37.3) — Highest risk; tenured customers likely had repeated payment failures

Q6 Does payment behavior influence churn?

Absolutely — it is the single strongest predictor of churn.
Customers with ≥1 failed payment: 29.6% churn rate (avg risk score 41.6)
Customers with zero failed payments: 5.2% churn rate (avg risk score 20.9)

That is a 5.7× difference. Failed payments account for $204,014 monthly expected loss — 74.4% of all revenue at risk. This strongly suggests implementing automated payment retry (dunning) would be the single highest-ROI retention investment.

Q7 Which customers are currently at high risk?

2,484 accounts (12.4% of the customer base) are classified as High or Critical risk:
• 270 Critical accounts (risk score ≥ 80): 83.6% average churn probability — these need immediate personal outreach within 48 hours
• 2,214 High accounts (risk score 60-79): 67.8% average churn probability — these need proactive campaign outreach this week

The top 5 highest-risk accounts are all Enterprise Monthly customers with payment failures and CSAT scores below 2.0, each representing $170+ monthly loss.

Q8 What is the potential revenue at risk?

$88,866 MRR ($1.07M ARR) is directly tied to High and Critical risk accounts. The probability-weighted expected monthly loss across the entire customer base is $274,093/month ($3.29M annually). This means even if we only save 30% of at-risk accounts, we protect approximately $320,000+ in annual revenue.

Q9 Which customers should the business prioritize?

Priority should follow the "Expected Monthly Loss" ranking (risk score × monthly fee), not just risk score alone. The exported high_risk_retention_list.csv provides the exact calling queue:

Tier 1 (Immediate — This Week): 270 Critical accounts → Personal phone call by Sr. CSM
Tier 2 (High Priority — Within 2 Weeks): 2,214 High accounts → Personalized email campaign + discount offer
Tier 3 (Monitor — Ongoing): 7,416 Medium accounts → Automated health check emails and in-app nudges

Q10 What actions could reduce churn?
Based on our analysis, here are the 4 highest-ROI retention interventions, ranked by projected revenue saved:
💳 #1: Automated Dunning & Payment Recovery — Save up to $102K/mo
Implement auto-retry at Days 3, 5, and 7 + pre-expiry card update reminders. Industry recovery rates: 30-50% of failed payments.
📅 #2: Monthly-to-Annual Conversion Campaign — Save up to $45K/mo
Offer "2 months free" for Annual upgrade. Even 15% conversion rate locks in 12-month retention for 2,085 accounts.
🎧 #3: CSAT Escalation & Support Quality Program — Save up to $19K/mo
Auto-escalate tickets with CSAT ≤ 2 to Senior Support within 4 hours. Issue service credits for poor experiences.
📉 #4: Usage Re-Engagement & Onboarding Program — Save up to $8K/mo
Trigger in-app walkthroughs and webinar invitations for accounts with declining usage trend ratios.
